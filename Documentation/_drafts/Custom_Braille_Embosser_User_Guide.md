## 1. Introduction

The **Custom Braille Embosser** is a small, hand-operated device that imprints permanent, raised, Grade-1 or Grade-2 braille onto card stock. You squeeze the handle, and a sheet of card is rolled between two cylindrical plates - an *embossing plate* with raised dots and a *universal counter plate* with matching cavities. The dots are pressed into the card permanently.

The plates are interchangeable: a different cylindrical pair gives you a different braille message. New plates are generated by entering text into one of the companion programs (see Section 4: Compatibility).

This guide is for **users of an already-built and already-assembled** embosser. If you are *building* one from scratch, refer to the Maker Guide.

For a transcript-backed written walkthrough of the three tutorial videos, see `Video_Tutorials/Custom_Braille_Embosser_Written_Tutorial.md`.

## 4. Compatibility

The Custom Braille Embosser is **plate-driven**: the device itself does not contain any text. Each braille message is encoded in its own pair of 3D-printed cylindrical plates:

- **Embossing plate** (raised dots) - sits inside the Embosser Holder A1/A2 pair in the Body Base.
- **Universal counter plate** (matching cavities) - sits inside the Counter Holder B1/B2 pair in the Body Top.

### 4.1 How to get more plates (two customizer options)

Two officially supported customizer options are available. **For most users, use the web-based customizer (primary).** The OpenSCAD version is an alternative for users who want to work offline or have full parametric control.

| Customizer | Use case | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary - Web-Based** | Most users; auto-translates English to Grade 1 / Grade 2 braille (50+ languages); works on any browser, including screen-reader users; no install. | <https://braille-card-and-cylinder-stl-gener.vercel.app> |
| **Alternative - OpenSCAD (offline)** | Offline use; full parametric control; existing OpenSCAD/CAD workflows. Included locally in this repo at [`Build_Files/3D_Printing_Files/Embossing_Plates_Customizer/OpenSCAD_Offline_Alternative/`](../../Build_Files/3D_Printing_Files/Embossing_Plates_Customizer/OpenSCAD_Offline_Alternative). ### 4.2 Pre-generated example plates

Two pre-generated example pairs are included in `Build_Files/3D_Printing_Files/Embossing_Plates_Examples/`:
- `Embossing_Plate_Brennen_0.3.stl` + `Universal_Counter_Plate_0.3.stl` - for 0.3 mm card stock.
- `Embossing_Plate_Brennen_0.4.stl` + `Universal_Counter_Plate_0.4.stl` - for 0.4 mm card stock.

These are useful for confirming the embosser is built correctly before generating user-specific plates.

### 4.3 Card-stock matching - critical

> **Important:** the embossing plate and the counter plate must match the card-stock thickness you intend to use. Using a 0.3 mm counter plate with 0.4 mm card will produce shallow, ill-formed dots; using a 0.4 mm counter plate with 0.3 mm card may not produce dots at all.

> **Useful shortcut from the transcript:** when the braille text changes but the card thickness stays the same, you usually only need a new embossing plate. The universal counter plate can be reused for other messages at that same thickness.

## 5. How to Use

### 5.1 Set the start position
1. Turn the handle until the **indicator triangles on both cylinders are directly on top of each other**.
2. This is the starting position shown in Video 3.
3. Rotate the entire embosser so the rollers are **horizontal** before inserting the card.

### 5.2 Orient the card correctly
1. Use the indicator triangles as your guide. They point in the direction the braille will be embossed.
2. Orient the card so it rolls **from left to right**, with the printed information **facing up** in the direction the triangles point.
3. Lower or drop the card gently into the slot between the rollers.
4. Double-check that the card looks level before you emboss.

### 5.3 Emboss the card
1. Rotate the handle **forward about a half turn**.
2. If you want to inspect the braille while it is forming, you can flip the embosser partway through and look at the dots.
3. If needed, move the handle back and forth slightly to finish the dots, but **stop before going past the last dot**. Going too far can misalign the embossing.
4. Once the dots look correct, roll the card all the way through.

### 5.4 Remove and read the card
1. Pull the card out.
2. Run your fingers across the embossed area to confirm the dots are readable.
3. If the dots are too shallow, check that the cylinder pair matches the card thickness:
   - **0.4 mm** cylinders for standard business-card stock
   - **0.3 mm** cylinders for thinner card stock

### 5.5 Swap cylinder pairs when you want a different message
1. Pull the lid off the embosser. The handle can stay attached.
2. Remove the current cylinders and insert the replacement embossing / counter pair.
3. If a fresh cylinder pair is stuck in the base, carefully work it loose with a **small flathead screwdriver**.
4. Re-align the indicator triangles before embossing the next card.

## 6. Cleaning

- Wipe both plates with a **dry, soft cloth** to remove paper dust between sessions.
- For stubborn marks, use a cloth lightly dampened with isopropyl alcohol. Do **not** soak PLA or PETG parts.
- Avoid solvents such as acetone (will damage PLA-based parts).

## 7. Care

- Store the embosser at room temperature, out of direct sunlight (PLA softens above ~50 °C).
- Do not drop the device onto hard floors; the snap-fit joints can pop apart on impact (they can be re-snapped, but repeated impacts may stress the joints).
- Re-print and replace any cracked or worn part using the STL files in `Build_Files/3D_Printing_Files/STL/`.

## 8. Disposal

- All 3D-printed parts are PLA or PETG and can be **recycled with #7 (other) plastics** where municipal recycling accepts that stream, or sent to a filament-recycling service.
- The device contains no batteries, no electronics, no metal fasteners, no glue. There are no hazardous materials.
